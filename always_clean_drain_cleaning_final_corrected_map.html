Professional Drain Cleaning Methods We Use

Choose technique based on obstruction, location, severity, pipe condition and access; only claim equipment and methods provider performs.

Drain Plunging

A cup plunger may help with suitable sink or tub clogs, while a flange plunger is designed for toilets. Plunging is generally suited to minor, localized obstructions.

Drain Snaking

A flexible manual or motorized cable can break through or retrieve certain clogs in fixture drains and branch lines. Equipment and cable size depend on access and pipe conditions.

Drain Augering

A toilet auger or suitable motorized auger can address dense blockages where the equipment can safely pass through the fixture or line.

Hydro Jetting

Hydro jetting uses a pressurized water stream to remove suitable grease, sludge and deposits from pipe walls. Pipe condition and access should be assessed before this method is selected.

Mechanical Root Cutting

Mechanical root-cutting attachments can clear root intrusion from suitable sewer lines. If roots entered through a damaged joint or defect, they may return until the underlying issue is repaired.

P-Trap Removal and Cleaning

An accessible P-trap can hold sludge or small objects. Where appropriate, the trap can be removed, cleaned and reinstalled, followed by a leak check and confirmation that its water seal is restored.

Manual Drain Debris Removal

Remove visible hair, food and debris from strainers, covers, stoppers and accessible openings.

Enzyme-Based Drain Maintenance Treatments

Enzyme-based treatments may support maintenance for certain organic buildup, but they are not an immediate fix for severe blockages. Confirm whether this option is offered and suitable for the drain.

Signs You Need Professional Drain Cleaning

Drain symptoms can point to different issues. A symptom is a reason to investigate, not a diagnosis on its own.

1. Slow-Draining Water

Water lingers in sinks, showers and tubs; possible partial clog due to grease, hair, soap or pipe restriction.

2. Gurgling or Bubbling Drain Sounds

Unusual noises from drains or nearby fixtures; possible drainage or venting problems, not necessarily proof of a clog.

3. Unpleasant Drain and Sewer Odors

Organic residue, grease, dry or faulty trap and possible sewer-gas issue; cleaning does not fix every cause.

4. Recurring or Stubborn Drain Clogs

Recurring backups after plunging/snaking; grease, roots, mineral deposits, foreign objects or pipe defects.

5. Multiple Fixtures Backing Up

Sinks, showers, toilets and washer affect each other; shared branch or main-line restriction possible.

6. Water Backflow or Standing Wastewater

Wastewater rises into floor drains, tubs, showers or lower fixtures; urgency and safe avoidance of sewage.

7. Drain Flies or Other Pests Around Drains

Insects around wet organic debris or biofilm; not proof of pipe blockage; separate pest treatment may be needed.

What Causes Clogged Drains?

Clogs can develop when household waste, deposits, foreign objects or tree roots restrict the passage of wastewater. The cause and location should be assessed before choosing a method.

Hair and Personal Care Residue

Hair tangles with personal care residue around bathroom stoppers, showers and tubs.

Soap Scum and Bath Product Buildup

Soap, shampoo, conditioner and bath residue collect around traps and drain walls.

Cooking Grease, Fats, and Oils

Grease adheres inside kitchen drains and retains debris; explain buildup and restricted flow.

Food Scraps and Garbage Disposal Waste

Coffee grounds, rice, pasta, fibrous vegetables and kitchen food waste obstruct sinks and disposal discharge.

Toilet Paper, Wipes, and Hygiene Products

Excess paper, wet wipes, cotton, dental floss and non-flushable hygiene products obstruct toilet waste lines.

Laundry Lint and Detergent Residue

Textile fibers, lint and detergent buildup in laundry standpipes, traps and utility drains.

Tree Root Intrusion

Roots enter underground sewer lines through joints or defects; recurring blockages may result.

Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Cleaning in Los Angeles

These answers address common booking and diagnostic questions. Confirm current availability, pricing, credentials and coverage with Always Clean before relying on them.

How much does professional drain cleaning cost in Los Angeles?

The cost depends on the drain type, blockage location and severity, access, pipe condition and cleaning method. Ask for an inspection or estimate process, including any diagnostic, emergency or after-hours fees, before approving work.

How long does drain cleaning usually take?

Timing varies with the location and severity of the blockage, access to the line, pipe condition and equipment required. The technician should provide a time estimate after assessing the issue.

Do you offer same-day drain cleaning in Los Angeles?

Same-day appointments depend on technician availability, location and job requirements. Contact Always Clean to confirm current scheduling; do not assume same-day service is guaranteed.

What types of drains do you clean?

The service scope may include kitchen and bathroom sinks, showers, bathtubs, toilets, laundry drains, floor drains, branch lines and main drains. Confirm the specific fixture and service availability when booking.

What is the difference between drain cleaning and drain clearing?

Drain clearing opens a path through a blockage. More thorough cleaning aims to remove deposits from the pipe walls as well. The appropriate scope depends on the cause and condition of the line.

Is hydro jetting better than drain snaking?

Neither method is best for every clog. Snaking can break through or retrieve many localized obstructions. Hydro jetting can remove suitable deposits from pipe walls, but requires an assessment of pipe condition and access.

Can professional drain cleaning remove tree roots?

Suitable root-cutting equipment can clear roots from a line, but it does not repair the joint or defect that allowed roots to enter. Roots may return if the underlying issue remains.

Why does my drain keep clogging after cleaning?

Recurring clogs can relate to grease, roots, foreign objects, scale, pipe defects or drainage slope. A camera inspection may help when symptoms persist or return.

How do I know whether my main sewer line is clogged?

Warning signs can include several fixtures backing up together, wastewater appearing in lower drains or one fixture affecting another. These symptoms need assessment and are not a diagnosis by themselves.

Do you need to inspect a drain with a camera before cleaning it?

No. A camera is useful for some deep, recurring or uncertain problems, but many localized clogs can be assessed without one. The need depends on symptoms, access and the suspected cause.

Can drain cleaning damage old pipes?

Yes, an unsuitable method or equipment can damage deteriorated piping. Pipe condition, material, access and the obstruction should be considered before choosing a cleaning technique.

What should I do if sewage is backing up into my home?

Stop using affected plumbing fixtures, keep people and pets away from wastewater, avoid direct contact and request urgent professional assessment. Do not attempt to handle sewage without appropriate protection.

How often should drains be professionally cleaned?

There is no universal interval. Maintenance depends on usage, pipe condition, history of recurring clogs and the type of property. Follow recommendations based on the actual drainage system.

Is professional drain cleaning better than chemical drain cleaners?

Professional assessment can identify the location and likely cause and select a targeted method. Chemical products may not resolve deeper or recurring problems and can create handling risks; follow product safety instructions.
